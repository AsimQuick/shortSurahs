# Local authority math ends prayer-time drift

The app's prayer times were wrong mainly because the old request hard-coded `method=2` (ISNA, which puts Fajr and Isha at 15° below the horizon) for every user, while the UAE's official timetable uses 18.2° plus fixed precautionary minutes. On 7 November 2026 that made Dubai's Fajr **14 minutes late** and Isha **15 minutes early**, with Dhuhr and Maghrib 3–4 minutes early. In late June the Fajr error grew to 17 minutes, so anyone using the app for suhoor would have kept eating after dawn. Two smaller errors added to this. Every UAE device reports `Asia/Dubai`, so users in Abu Dhabi (+4 min) and Fujairah (−6 min) silently got Dubai's times. Outside the Gulf, using the timezone's city as the location fails badly: a Mumbai user on `Asia/Kolkata` is 62 minutes off at Dhuhr, and a bare `UTC` zone geocoded to Tokyo. For a worldwide, offline-first app with no GPS, the best fix has four parts. Calculate on the device with adhan-js. Pick parameters from a per-country table of religious authorities, with Hanafi Asr set explicitly for South Asia and the high-latitude rule chosen by latitude. Take coordinates from a bundled city that matches the device's timezone, and let the user pick a different city. Schedule local notifications in a rolling 12-day window. For the UAE, use 18.2°/18.2° with offsets of sunrise −3.5, Dhuhr +3, Asr +1.25 and Maghrib +3.5 minutes, calculated at Dubai and then shifted per emirate. Against the published November timetable, this matched to the minute on 28–30 of 30 days per prayer and was never more than one minute off. The app now implements this design. The UAE offsets were fitted to one month of data, and several countries still have no agreed method.

## ISNA's 15° angle accounts for most of the error

The old call was `GET /v1/timingsByCity/{date}?city={timezone city}&country=&method=2`. Comparing it with the official Dubai times for one day shows how big the error was and what caused it.

| Dubai, 7 Nov 2026 | Fajr | Sunrise | Dhuhr | Asr | Maghrib | Isha |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Official timetable (Khaleej Times) | 05:11 | 06:26 | 12:05 | 15:13 | 17:38 | 18:54 |
| Old request (ISNA, method 2) | 05:25 | 06:30 | 12:02 | 15:12 | 17:34 | 18:39 |
| **Old request error (min)** | **+14** | **+4** | **−3** | **−1** | **−4** | **−15** |
| Aladhan method 16 at Dubai coordinates | 05:11 | 06:30 | 12:05 | 15:13 | 17:38 | 18:54 |
| adhan-js `Dubai()` preset | 05:11 | 06:27 | 12:05 | 15:15 | 17:38 | 18:54 |

Sources for the table: official times are from the [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/prayer-time-uae) data for that date. The ISNA row is from the same [Aladhan](https://api.aladhan.com/v1/timingsByCity/07-11-2026?city=Dubai&country=AE&method=16) request run with `method=2`. The method 16 row is from [Aladhan by coordinates](https://api.aladhan.com/v1/timings/07-11-2026?latitude=25.077&longitude=55.309&method=16&timezonestring=Asia/Dubai). The adhan-js row was computed locally with adhan-js 4.4.6 at the same point (25.077, 55.309).

The errors come from several independent sources, and each can be measured. **The twilight angle is the largest.** At 25°N, ISNA's 15° puts Fajr 14 minutes later and Isha 15 minutes earlier than 18.2° ([Aladhan](https://api.aladhan.com/v1/timings/15-11-2026?latitude=25.2048&longitude=55.2708&method=2)). The gap grows in summer. On 27 June 2026, Khaleej Times showed Dubai Fajr at 04:01 ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/voices-of-the-uae-influencers-creators-on-why-they-feel-safe-seen-and-supported)), and ISNA gave **04:18** ([Aladhan](https://api.aladhan.com/v1/timingsByCity/27-06-2026?city=Dubai&country=AE&method=2)). The second source is **precautionary minutes**. Dhuhr, Maghrib and Sunrise do not depend on the angle, yet the official table is 3 minutes later at Dhuhr, 4 later at Maghrib and 4 earlier at Sunrise. No generic method adds those offsets. The third source is **location substitution**. With ISNA, Abu Dhabi runs 2–5 minutes later than Dubai ([Aladhan](https://api.aladhan.com/v1/timings/15-11-2026?latitude=24.4539&longitude=54.3773&method=2)). In wide timezones the error is far larger: Mumbai versus Kolkata is **62 minutes** at Dhuhr, Detroit versus New York 36, Jeddah versus Riyadh 30, and Glasgow versus London 17 ([Aladhan](https://api.aladhan.com/v1/timings/15-11-2026?latitude=19.076&longitude=72.8777&method=2)). Two further sources did not affect the UAE but matter elsewhere. **Hanafi Asr** is about 45 minutes later than the standard (Shafi) Asr in Dubai ([Aladhan](https://api.aladhan.com/v1/timings/15-11-2026?latitude=25.2048&longitude=55.2708&method=16&school=1)). **High-latitude rules** move London's Fajr on 21 June anywhere between 01:16 and 03:40 ([Aladhan](https://api.aladhan.com/v1/timings/21-06-2026?latitude=51.5074&longitude=-0.1278&method=2&latitudeAdjustmentMethod=1)). Rounding to whole minutes adds at most one minute.

The geocoding step also made the request hard to check. Aladhan's spec marks `country` as required but accepts an empty string. Without `timezonestring`, it returns times in the timezone of the geocoded place, not the device's timezone ([OpenAPI spec](https://api.aladhan.com/v1/documentation/openapi/prayer-times/yaml)). For major cities the empty country did no harm. However, the response's `meta.latitude/longitude` came back as the placeholders **8.8888888 / 7.7777777** ([Aladhan](https://api.aladhan.com/v1/timingsByCity/15-11-2026?city=Dubai&country=&method=2)), so the app could never log or verify the point it was actually given. Non-city zone names are a real hazard: **`city=UTC` resolved to Asia/Tokyo and `city=GMT` to Africa/Banjul** ([Aladhan](https://api.aladhan.com/v1/timingsByCity/15-11-2026?city=UTC&country=&method=2)), which affects emulators and some Android setups. Hard-coding `method=2` also bypassed Aladhan's own location-based default, which would have chosen method 16 for the UAE ([Aladhan](https://api.aladhan.com/v1/timingsByCity/15-11-2026?city=Dubai&country=)). None of the API bugs reported by the community explain these numbers. As one forum reply puts it, "if you pass the wrong co-ordinates or the wrong method or wrong parameters, you'll get the wrong timings" ([community.islamic.network](https://community.islamic.network/d/142-timing-in-the-api)).

## The UAE timetable is Dubai astronomy plus fixed minute offsets

> **Correction after a field check (7 Nov 2026, evening).** The Khaleej Times table this section fits against runs **5 minutes later than Awqaf for Fajr**. The owner's Dubai mosque and Muslim Mate showed Fajr at 05:06 for 8 Nov 2026, where this fit gives 05:11. Awqaf's published Dubai Fajr for 3 Feb 2026 (05:37, Abu Dhabi 05:41) shows the same 5-minute gap. The app now adds a Fajr offset of −5 minutes to the parameters below (equivalent within one minute, all year, to a 19.25° Fajr angle). The other prayers are unchanged.


No primary Awqaf or IACAD document gives the angles. The official site is rendered by JavaScript and gave no methodology ([awqaf.gov.ae](https://www.awqaf.gov.ae/?lang=en)), and third-party sites disagree. Some claim the UAE uses Umm al-Qura (18.5°, Isha 90 minutes after Maghrib) ([FivePrayer](https://www.fiveprayer.app/prayer-times/dubai)). The published data rules that out: in November, Isha is about 76 minutes after Maghrib, not 90. So the parameters were reverse-engineered by fitting unrounded 18.2°/18.2° times at Dubai (25.077, 55.309) to all 30 November days of the [Khaleej Times Dubai table](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/prayer-time-uae/dubai). The fit gave **Fajr 0 offset (exact on 30/30 days), Isha 0 (29/30), Dhuhr +3 (29/30), Sunrise −3.53 on average (range −4.00 to −2.97), Maghrib +3.46 (+2.97 to +3.90), and Asr +1.18 (+0.67 to +1.80)**. Fajr and Isha land on exactly zero only at Dubai. At Abu Dhabi coordinates they drift (+0.96 and −0.20 on average), which shows the base table is calculated once, for Dubai. The ±3.5-minute Sunrise and Maghrib offsets most likely combine a 3-minute safety margin (tamkin) with a slightly different refraction model. The Asr residual of about 1.2 minutes points to a different Asr formula rather than a round-number offset. This also explains why the two "Dubai" methods disagree. Aladhan's raw Asr comes out about 1.3 minutes later than adhan-js's for the same inputs. Aladhan's method 16 adds no Asr offset and matches ([Aladhan](https://api.aladhan.com/v1/timings/07-11-2026?latitude=25.077&longitude=55.309&method=16&timezonestring=Asia/Dubai)). The adhan-js `Dubai()` preset adds +3 to Asr and is **2 minutes late every day** ([adhan-js source](https://github.com/batoulapps/adhan-js/blob/master/src/CalculationMethod.ts)).

| Exact-minute matches, Nov 2026 (30 days) | Fajr | Sunrise | Dhuhr | Asr | Maghrib | Isha |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| adhan-js `Dubai()` as shipped | 30 | 13 | 29 | **0** | 18 | 29 |
| `Dubai()` with `adjustments.asr = −2` | 30 | 13 | 29 | 24 | 18 | 29 |
| **18.2°/18.2°, methodAdjustments sunrise −3.5, dhuhr +3, asr +1.25, maghrib +3.5** | **30** | **28** | **29** | **28** | **28** | **29** |

These counts were computed with adhan-js 4.4.6, which accepts fractional-minute adjustments, against the [Khaleej Times Dubai table](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/prayer-time-uae/dubai).

Emirates are handled the same way, which goes against the usual intuition. The official tables do not recalculate for each city. They shift the Dubai table by a whole number of minutes, applied to all six prayers. Khaleej Times shows **Abu Dhabi as Dubai +4** and **Fujairah as Dubai −6** for every prayer on 1, 7, 15 and 30 November ([KT Abu Dhabi](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/prayer-time-uae/abu-dhabi); [KT Fujairah](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/prayer-time-uae/fujairah)). Astronomy alone gives Fujairah about −4. As a result, running adhan-js at Fujairah's own coordinates is 2–3 minutes late on every prayer ([KT Fujairah](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/prayer-time-uae/fujairah)). For matching mosques, a user's exact coordinates are *less* accurate than Dubai plus the official shift. The only other option that came close was Aladhan method 16 with `tune` correcting Sunrise by −4, and that was tested on a single day. Method 8 ("Gulf Region", 19.5°, Isha 90 minutes) put Fajr 6 minutes early and Isha 11 minutes late ([Aladhan](https://api.aladhan.com/v1/timings/07-11-2026?latitude=25.077&longitude=55.309&method=8&timezonestring=Asia/Dubai)). The only recent official change found was Friday prayer moving to 12:45 nationwide from 2 January 2026 ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/uae-to-change-friday-prayer-timings-from-january-2026)). That is a fixed congregational time, not a change to the calculation.

## A country table beats Aladhan's nearest-anchor guess

Aladhan picks a default method by finding the authority whose anchor point is nearest the user. This works in the Gulf but fails in several countries. Germany and Belgium get France's 12° method, Italy and Bosnia get Tunisia's, Australia and New Zealand get Indonesia's 20°, South Africa and Kenya get Umm al-Qura, Nigeria and Senegal get Morocco's, and Uzbekistan gets Tehran's. The default Asr school was also Shafi even for Pakistan ([Aladhan /v1/methods](https://api.aladhan.com/v1/methods); [Aladhan probe](https://api.aladhan.com/v1/timingsByCity/07-11-2026?city=London&country=GB)). No major app publishes its own mapping. The best public example is a `countries.json` proposed for adhan-swift, which was never merged ([adhan-swift PR #72](https://github.com/batoulapps/adhan-swift/pull/72)). The adhan maintainer's guidance is to map only countries where the method is well established, keep the madhab optional, choose the high-latitude rule by latitude rather than by country, and "definitely default to Moon Sighting Committee" for the UK ([adhan-swift #41](https://github.com/batoulapps/adhan-swift/issues/41)). With those principles, the research supports this table:

Several rows depart from the library presets, and those departures matter. **Malaysia moved Subuh from 20° to 18° nationwide after the November 2019 MKI ruling, about 8 minutes later** ([Berita Harian](https://www.bharian.com.my/amp/berita/nasional/2019/11/633306/bhplus); [NST](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/news/nation/2020/02/563370/8-minutes-added-subuh-prayer-time-penang-tomorrow)), so both libraries' Malaysia presets are now 8 minutes early. Indonesia's Kemenag reaffirmed 20° in December 2020, although Muhammadiyah uses 18° ([NU Online](https://www.nu.or.id/nasional/kemenag-kriteria-waktu-subuh-20-derajat-benar-secara-fiqih-dan-sains-fDnQZ)). Diyanet has used 18° for imsak since 1982–83 ([takvim.com.tr](https://www.takvim.com.tr/Guncel/2013/07/17/diyanetten-imsak-vakti-aciklamasi-1374068706)), and adhan-js calls its own Turkey method "an approximation of the Diyanet method". Morocco's corrections come from a third-party site ([bladi.net](https://www.bladi.net/horaires-priere/maroc/marrakech.html)), although Aladhan's method 21 applies the same Dhuhr and Maghrib +5 ([Aladhan](https://api.aladhan.com/v1/timingsByCity/07-11-2026?city=Rabat&country=MA)). Umm al-Qura's Isha becomes 120 minutes after Maghrib in Ramadan, and adhan-js leaves that as a manual +30 ([adhan-js METHODS.md](https://github.com/batoulapps/adhan-js/blob/master/METHODS.md)). Hanafi Asr is the convention in Pakistan, India, Bangladesh and Afghanistan ([salahtimes](https://www.salahtimes.com/india/settings); [praytimes.org](https://praytimes.org/docs/methods)), but neither library applies it automatically.

For high latitudes, `HighLatitudeRule.recommended(coords)` returns SeventhOfTheNight above 48° and MiddleOfTheNight below ([adhan-js source](https://github.com/batoulapps/adhan-js/blob/master/src/HighLatitudeRule.ts)). Aladhan's default is angle-based instead, so the two libraries will disagree in summer in the UK, Ireland, the Netherlands, Germany, Canada and Russia. The adhan authors' rule is defensible, but users who compare against Aladhan-backed sites will notice the difference. In the installed 4.4.6 code, `polarCircleResolution` defaults to `Unresolved`, which leaves times invalid where the sun does not set or rise. Setting `AqrabYaum` above about 65° avoids that ([adhan-js README](https://github.com/batoulapps/adhan-js#readme)).

## A bundled city list replaces GPS without loss of accuracy

Solar times shift about 4 minutes per degree of longitude, so the coordinates need to be accurate to roughly a city, not to the metre. The device timezone can be read with no permission through `expo-localization` (`getCalendars()[0].timeZone`) ([Expo docs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v55.0.0/sdk/localization/)). The timezone alone is too coarse, though. IANA's public-domain `zone1970.tab` gives each zone one "principal location" and merges countries: `AE,OM,…` share Asia/Dubai, and `SA,AQ,KW,YE` share Asia/Riyadh ([zone1970.tab](https://data.iana.org/time-zones/tzdb/zone1970.tab)). Taken naively, this puts Muscat users about 13 minutes off and leaves parts of `America/New_York` more than an hour off. It also hides the country, and the country is what selects the calculation method. The fix is GeoNames' **cities15000** dataset (~25,000 places with population over 15,000 or capitals). Each row has WGS84 coordinates, an IANA timezone ID, a country code and a region code, and the dataset is licensed **CC BY 4.0** ([GeoNames readme](https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/readme.txt)). To find a default location, filter the bundled list by the device's timezone. That gives a city and a country code with no prompt. A city picker then covers users who live far from their zone's main city. Attribution can be a single credits line ([CC BY 4.0 §3](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en)).

The alternatives undermine the privacy pillar. expo-location's geocoding on Android requires `requestForegroundPermissionsAsync` first ([Expo Location](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v55.0.0/sdk/location/)). On iOS, `CLGeocoder` is a rate-limited network service and is deprecated in iOS 26 ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/clgeocoder)). Hosted APIs are no better. Aladhan is the only well-documented free option, and every call sends the user's location and IP address to a third party, with a limit of 12 requests per second ([Aladhan headers](https://api.aladhan.com/v1/calendar/2026/12?latitude=25.2048&longitude=55.2708&method=16)). Mawaqit's times are entered per mosque, and its terms prohibit commercial use ([mawaqit.net](https://mawaqit.net/)). On the other side, adhan-js 4.4.6 (released 31 August 2026) is MIT-licensed, has **zero dependencies and is 4.66 kB gzipped** ([Bundlephobia](https://bundlephobia.com/package/adhan@4.4.6); [npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/adhan)). It uses Meeus's astronomical algorithms, and its CI tests it against official timetables, including Dubai against awqaf.gov.ae to within ±1 minute ([times.test.ts](https://github.com/batoulapps/adhan-js/blob/master/test/times.test.ts)). It returns absolute `Date` instants and reads only the year, month and day from its input date ([README](https://github.com/batoulapps/adhan-js#readme)). That makes it a natural fit for both offline display and notification scheduling.

One UAE detail follows from this design. Every UAE device reports Asia/Dubai, so the timezone default always resolves to Dubai. Users in Abu Dhabi or Fujairah get their +4 or −6 shift only after choosing their city.

## A 12-day rolling window stays under iOS's 64-notification cap

iOS "keeps the soonest-firing 64 notifications ... and discards the rest" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uilocalnotification)). At five prayers a day, 12 days (60 notifications) is the most that fits safely. Android has a quieter failure. Exact alarms on Android 12+ require the user-granted, revocable `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` permission, which is "not pre-granted to fresh installs of apps targeting Android 13". Without it, inexact alarms fire "within one hour of the supplied trigger time" ([Android developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/alarms/schedule)). expo-notifications SDK 55 falls back automatically. It calls `setExactAndAllowWhileIdle` only when `canScheduleExactAlarms()` is true and otherwise uses `setAndAllowWhileIdle` ([ExpoSchedulingDelegate.kt](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/sdk-55/packages/expo-notifications/android/src/main/java/expo/modules/notifications/service/delegates/ExpoSchedulingDelegate.kt)). Its manifest does not declare `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` ([AndroidManifest.xml](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/main/packages/expo-notifications/android/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml)), so the app must add it in `app.json`. Expo also has no JavaScript check for the permission, so the app can only explain the need and deep-link the user to the system's "Alarms & reminders" setting. `USE_EXACT_ALARM` would avoid the prompt, but Google Play restricts it to alarm and calendar apps that need precisely timed actions, and apps that do not qualify are "disallowed from publishing" ([Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13161072)).

Scheduling should go in this order. First create the Android channel, because on Android 13 the permission prompt does not appear until a channel exists ([Expo Notifications](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v55.0.0/sdk/notifications/)). Then request permission. Then cancel all scheduled notifications and schedule one-shot `DATE` triggers directly from adhan's `Date` instants. Rebuild this window on launch, whenever the app returns to the foreground, and whenever the location or method changes. Because DATE triggers fire once, reminders stop if the app goes unopened for 12 days. That is the main trade-off of a design with no server.

## What the app now ships, and where it is still unverified

Based on this research, the app now calculates times on the device with adhan-js 4.4.6 and no longer calls Aladhan. For the UAE, it uses 18.2°/18.2° with methodAdjustments of sunrise −3.5, Dhuhr +3, Asr +1.25 and Maghrib +3.5. It calculates at Dubai's coordinates and shifts the result per emirate: Abu Dhabi +4, Fujairah −6, and otherwise the longitude difference from Dubai × 4 minutes, rounded. A country-to-method table in `services/prayerCalculation.ts` follows the mapping above, with Hanafi Asr for PK, IN, BD and AF and `HighLatitudeRule.recommended` everywhere. The default location is the bundled GeoNames city for the device's timezone, needs no permission, and can be changed in a city picker. Local notifications use expo-notifications. They are scheduled 12 days ahead (60 at most) and rolled forward every time the app comes to the foreground. `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` is declared for Android. These are implementation facts, not field-validated accuracy claims.

The known gaps are specific. **The UAE offsets were fitted on November data only.** Summer brings a different solar declination and Asr shadow geometry, so the fractional offsets need re-checking against a June or July table. **Saudi Arabia's Ramadan Isha +30 is not applied**, so during Ramadan, Isha in Saudi Arabia will show 30 minutes earlier than the official calendar. **On Android 14 and later, users must grant exact alarms themselves.** Until they do, reminders can arrive up to an hour late. **OM, CA, US and FR are contested choices.** Oman uses Qatar's method in the app, while Aladhan's default picks Dubai's. Canada uses Moonsighting, while Aladhan uses ISNA. The US uses ISNA, while the adhan authors prefer Moonsighting. France uses MWL 18°, while UOIF uses 12°. The research also leaves some smaller items open. Official shifts for Sharjah, Ajman, Umm Al Quwain, Ras Al Khaimah, Al Ain and the Western Region were never collected, and the longitude fallback can be about 2 minutes off, as Fujairah's −4 versus −6 shows. Khaleej Times does not name Awqaf as its source. The implemented table also maps PS and LB to Egyptian, which this research did not examine. Finally, the code leaves `polarCircleResolution` at its default, `Unresolved`.

## Conclusion

The main lesson is that "accurate" here means matching the published timetable, not matching the sky. The UAE table is an 18.2° calculation for one point, adjusted by hand and shifted per emirate. Running that model at Dubai beat running the same angles at each user's real coordinates. Not collecting GPS is therefore better for both privacy and accuracy, provided the app knows the user's country and city. The old failure was a parameter choice, not an API defect, so the same care applies to any source: an on-device library with the wrong country row would be just as wrong.

Moving the calculation onto the device also changes where the remaining risk sits. The calculation is now deterministic and testable offline. What remains is data staleness, because the offsets are fitted to one month and authorities do change angles, as Malaysia did in 2019. Delivery is the other risk, because Android exact-alarm permission and the 12-day expiry decide whether a correct time ever reaches the user. The highest-value next steps are a regression test against published timetables for the top user countries across a full year, starting with a summer UAE month and a Saudi Ramadan, and an in-app prompt for exact alarms on Android.
